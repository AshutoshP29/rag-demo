
The Amazon Rainforest is the world's largest tropical rainforest, covering approximately 5.5 million square kilometres across South America. It spans nine countries, with about 60% of the forest located in Brazil.

The Amazon is home to nearly 10% of the world's known species of plants and animals. It also plays a crucial role in regulating the Earth's climate by absorbing large amounts of carbon dioxide and producing oxygen through photosynthesis.

Deforestation, illegal mining, and wildfires threaten the health of the rainforest. Conservation efforts include establishing protected areas, enforcing environmental laws, and supporting indigenous communities who have lived in the region for centuries.
